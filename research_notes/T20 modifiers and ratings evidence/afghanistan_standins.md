# Afghanistan stand-ins, men's T20 World Cup 2010–2026

Purpose: for each men's T20 World Cup Afghanistan played in, which team would have taken their place had Afghanistan not been in the field. Used to build a faithful "no-Afghanistan" counterfactual field per edition.

Rule applied (in order of preference):

1. If Afghanistan came through a global qualifier: the stand-in is the team that lost the qualifier final to them, or, if both finalists qualified, the highest-placed team in that qualifier that did NOT qualify.
2. If Afghanistan qualified automatically (rankings or prior-edition finish): the stand-in is the next team that would have received that automatic slot; if that team was already in the field by another route, the slot cascades to the next team from the relevant qualifier.

Researched 28 Oct 2026. Sources are Wikipedia tournament pages (standings tables), ICC press pages and contemporary reporting, all read-only.

## Per-edition notes and evidence

### 2010 (West Indies) — stand-in: United Arab Emirates

- Qualifier: 2010 ICC World Twenty20 Qualifier, UAE, Feb 2010. Eight teams, two groups of four, Super Four, final. "The top two teams progressed to play in the 2010 ICC World Twenty20."
- Final: Afghanistan beat Ireland by 8 wickets. "Both finalists qualified for the 2010 ICC World Twenty20 – Afghanistan for Group C and Ireland for Group D."
- Super Four table: 1 Ireland 4 pts (NRR 1.233), 2 Afghanistan 4 pts (0.100), 3 UAE 2 pts (−0.244), 4 Netherlands 2 pts (−1.105). No third-place play-off existed.
- Rule 1 (both finalists qualified): next is the highest-placed non-qualifier = UAE (3rd). UAE also beat Netherlands head-to-head in Group B (by 6 wickets, 10 Feb), so the 3rd/4th order is not just NRR.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_ICC_World_Twenty20_Qualifier

### 2012 (Sri Lanka) — stand-in: Namibia

- Qualifier: 2012 ICC World Twenty20 Qualifier, UAE, March 2012. 16 teams, two groups of eight; "only the top two teams from this event qualifying for the 2012 ICC World Twenty20."
- Group A: Afghanistan 1st (7/7). Group B: Namibia 1st (7/7), Ireland 2nd.
- Play-offs: Qualifying Final 1 Afghanistan beat Namibia by 47 runs; Elimination Semi-Final Ireland beat Netherlands by 7 wickets; Qualifying Final 2 Ireland beat Namibia by 9 wickets; Final Ireland beat Afghanistan by 5 wickets; 5th-place Scotland beat Canada by 4 wickets.
- Final standings: 1 Ireland, 2 Afghanistan, 3 Namibia, 4 Netherlands, 5 Scotland, 6 Canada. No 3rd-place match; Namibia are 3rd as loser of Qualifying Final 2.
- Rule 1 (both finalists qualified): next = 3rd = Namibia. Had Afghanistan been absent, Namibia (Group B winners, unbeaten in the group) would have been the second finalist in the natural bracket as well.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_ICC_World_Twenty20_Qualifier

### 2014 (Bangladesh) — stand-in: Scotland

- Qualifier: 2013 ICC World Twenty20 Qualifier, UAE, Nov 2013. 16 teams. "The top 6 nations (previously 2) qualified for the 2014 ICC World Twenty20": Ireland, Afghanistan, Netherlands, UAE, Nepal, Hong Kong.
- Afghanistan: Group B 1st (12 pts); semi-final beat Nepal by 7 wickets; lost the final to Ireland by 68 runs. Final standings: 1 Ireland, 2 Afghanistan, 3 Nepal, 4 UAE, 5 Netherlands, 6 Hong Kong, 7 Scotland, 8 Papua New Guinea.
- 7th-place play-off (29 Nov): "Scotland won by 5 wickets" (146/5 v 143/5) over PNG.
- Rule 1 (six qualified): next = 7th = Scotland.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_ICC_World_Twenty20_Qualifier

### 2016 (India) — stand-in: Namibia

- Qualifier: 2015 ICC World Twenty20 Qualifier, Ireland and Scotland, July 2015. 14 teams; top six qualified: Scotland, Netherlands (shared title, final abandoned), Ireland, Hong Kong, Afghanistan, Oman.
- Afghanistan: Group B 3rd (8 pts); lost Qualifier 1 to Hong Kong by 5 wickets; beat PNG by 6 wickets in Qualifier 3 (which carried a World T20 spot); beat Oman by 5 wickets in the 5th-place play-off. Finished 5th.
- Final standings per Wikipedia: 1 Scotland, 2 Netherlands, 3 Ireland, 4 Hong Kong, 5 Afghanistan, 6 Oman, 7 Namibia, 8 Papua New Guinea. There was no 7th-place play-off; Namibia lost Qualifier 2 (to Netherlands) and Qualifier 4 (to Oman, the last qualifying spot), PNG lost Qualifier 3 (to Afghanistan). Namibia are placed above PNG on group-stage position (Namibia 3rd in Group A, PNG 4th, both 7 pts, Namibia better NRR).
- Rule 1 (six qualified): next = 7th = Namibia. Ambiguity: PNG are the side Afghanistan directly knocked out of a qualifying spot in Qualifier 3, so a "who did Afghanistan eliminate" reading gives PNG. The stated rule (highest-placed non-qualifier) gives Namibia.
- Source: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_ICC_World_Twenty20_Qualifier (plus Gulf Times report on the Oman v Namibia and Afghanistan v PNG play-offs)

### 2021 (UAE/Oman, originally Australia 2020) — stand-in: United Arab Emirates (First Round)

- Automatic route: "As of 31 December 2018, the top nine ranked ICC Full Members, alongside hosts India [sic — the 2021 edition was transferred to UAE/Oman; the original rule was hosts Australia + top nine], qualified directly." Top eight of those went straight to the Super 12s; Sri Lanka (9th) and Bangladesh (10th) went to the First Round.
- Rankings at the 31 Dec 2018 cut-off (table on the 2019 Qualifier page): 1 Pakistan 138, 2 India 126, 3 England 118, 4 Australia 117, 5 South Africa 114, 6 New Zealand 112, 7 West Indies 101, 8 Afghanistan 92, 9 Sri Lanka 87, 10 Bangladesh 77, 11 Scotland 62, 12 Zimbabwe 55, 13 UAE 50, 14 Netherlands 50, 15 Hong Kong 42, 16 Oman 39, 17 Ireland 34. Dawn (Jan 2019) confirms Afghanistan "rose to eighth" and SL/Bangladesh "ranked ninth and 10th respectively at the 2018 cut-off date of Dec 31".
- Counterfactual: without Afghanistan, Sri Lanka (9th) moves into the Super 12 direct slots and Scotland (11th) becomes the ninth direct qualifier, joining Bangladesh in the First Round. Scotland would then not have played the 2019 Qualifier.
- 2019 ICC T20 World Cup Qualifier (UAE, Oct–Nov 2019) final standings: 1 Netherlands, 2 PNG, 3 Ireland, 4 Namibia, 5 Scotland, 6 Oman, 7 UAE, 8 Hong Kong, 9 Canada, 10 Jersey, 11 Kenya, 12 Singapore, 13 Bermuda, 14 Nigeria. Six qualified. Scotland beat Oman by 5 wickets in the 5th-place play-off; UAE lost Qualifier 1 to Netherlands (8 wkts) and Qualifier 3 to Scotland (90 runs).
- Rule 2 cascade: Scotland's Qualifier place is vacated, so the next team is 7th-placed UAE (also the qualifier hosts). Net stand-in in the 16-team field: UAE in the First Round.
- Note on Zimbabwe (12th at cut-off): irrelevant to this cascade and in any case Zimbabwe Cricket was suspended by the ICC in July 2019 and Nigeria replaced them in the 2019 Qualifier.
- Sources: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_ICC_T20_World_Cup_Qualifier ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ICC_Men%27s_T20_World_Cup ; https://www.dawn.com/news/1454911/afghanistan-earn-direct-entry-to-t20-world-cup ; https://bulawayo24.com/index-id-sports-sc-cricket-byo-152727.html (Zimbabwe 12th); https://www.wisden.com/cricket-news/namibia-and-nigeria-replace-zimbabwe-in-t20-world-cup-qualifiers

### 2022 (Australia) — stand-in: Papua New Guinea (First Round), with Sri Lanka promoted to Super 12

- Automatic route (Sky Sports, Nov 2021): "winners and runners-up of the 2021 World Cup plus the next six highest-ranked teams progress straight through", rankings cut-off 15 Nov 2021. Direct: Australia, England, India, Pakistan, New Zealand, South Africa, Afghanistan, Bangladesh. First Round: West Indies, Sri Lanka, Scotland, Namibia (the other four 2021 Super 12 sides) plus four Global Qualifier teams. Sky Sports gives Bangladesh 8th, Sri Lanka 9th, West Indies 10th (Afghanistan 7th per contemporary reports). Neither SL (last match 4 Nov) nor WI (last match 6 Nov) played between those reports and the 15 Nov cut-off, so the order held.
- Step 1 (automatic slot): without Afghanistan the next of the six highest-ranked is Sri Lanka (9th), who move from First Round to Super 12.
- Step 2 (vacated First Round slot): the First Round automatic places were the remaining 2021 Super 12 sides; the next team in line by rankings is the highest-ranked outsider. The ICC's own Global Qualifier allocation named Zimbabwe, Nepal, UAE and Singapore as the "highest ranked teams not already qualified" (Nov 2021), with Zimbabwe the highest of those (11th at the time, per Zimbabwe-focused reporting; 1 Sep 2022 table has them 11th on 194). Zimbabwe would therefore take the First Round slot directly.
- Step 3 (cascade): Zimbabwe actually qualified anyway by winning Global Qualifier B (beat Netherlands by 37 runs in the final). With Zimbabwe out of Qualifier B, the next team there is 3rd-placed Papua New Guinea (beat USA by 5 runs in the 3rd-place play-off; Qualifier B final standings: 1 Zimbabwe, 2 Netherlands, 3 PNG, 4 USA, 5 Uganda, 6 Hong Kong, 7 Jersey, 8 Singapore).
- Runner-up: Nepal, 3rd in Global Qualifier A (beat Oman by 9 wickets in the 3rd-place play-off; A standings: 1 UAE, 2 Ireland, 3 Nepal, 4 Oman). Choose Nepal if you model the extra slot as a single "best third place" pick across both qualifiers rather than following the Zimbabwe chain; Nepal were the higher-ranked of the two third-placed sides.
- Ambiguity flagged: the real 2021 Super 12 would also have had a different twelfth team without Afghanistan, which changes who the "remaining four" were. The chain above is the cleanest rule-based answer.
- Sources: https://www.skysports.com/cricket/news/18626/12462879/t20-world-cup-west-indies-and-sri-lanka-must-qualify-for-super-12-at-next-years-tournament ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_ICC_Men%27s_T20_World_Cup ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_ICC_Men%27s_T20_World_Cup_Global_Qualifier_A ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_ICC_Men%27s_T20_World_Cup_Global_Qualifier_B ; https://singaporecricket.org/singapore-earn-qualification-to-global-qualifier-for-2022-t20-world-cup-in-australia/

### 2024 (West Indies/USA) — stand-in: Zimbabwe (best supported); Italy as runner-up chain

- Automatic route (ICC pathway page): hosts West Indies and USA; top eight from 2022 (top four of each Super 12 group: India, England, Pakistan, South Africa, New Zealand, Australia, Sri Lanka, Netherlands); then Afghanistan and Bangladesh as "Next best on T20I rankings (as of 14 November 2022)". Post-tournament table (13–14 Nov 2022, CricketTimes repost): 7 West Indies 236, 8 Sri Lanka 235, 9 Bangladesh 222, 10 Afghanistan 217; positions 11+ not printed.
- Rule 2: the next team is whoever was 11th at the cut-off among teams not already in. Evidence for the 11th/12th order:
  - 1 Sep 2022 ICC table (Wikipedia, International cricket in 2022–23): 11 Zimbabwe 194, 12 Ireland 187, 13 UAE 185, 14 Namibia 183, 15 Scotland 182, 16 Nepal 180.
  - ICC preview (mid-2022) had Zimbabwe 12th before their Bangladesh series; they were 11th by September.
  - Between 1 Sep and 14 Nov 2022 both sides played only World Cup matches (Zimbabwe: W v Ireland, W v Scotland, W v West Indies, NR v SA, W v Pakistan, L v Bangladesh, L v Netherlands, L v India; Ireland: W v Scotland, L v Zimbabwe, W v West Indies, L v Sri Lanka, W v England, NR v Afghanistan, L v Australia, L v New Zealand). Applying the ICC rating rules to those results, Zimbabwe's rating rises more than Ireland's, so Zimbabwe should still be 11th at the cut-off. Wisden's later piece on Zimbabwe's 2024 miss frames it as Zimbabwe losing out on the "top eight" route to Netherlands/Bangladesh, consistent with them being the next team outside the twelve.
  - Not retrievable: the exact ICC table dated 14 Nov 2022 (the Wikipedia rankings template of that era was deleted; archive fetches blocked). Treat the Zimbabwe 11th call as well-supported but not primary-sourced.
- Cascade if the order were Ireland 11th: Ireland qualified anyway via the 2023 Europe Regional Final (1 Scotland 12 pts, 2 Ireland 9, 3 Italy 7, 4 Jersey 6), so the slot cascades to Italy.

### 2026 (India/Sri Lanka) — stand-in: Kenya (via Zimbabwe chain); Scotland as alternative

- Automatic route: hosts India and Sri Lanka; "the top seven teams from the 2024 tournament" excluding hosts (Afghanistan, Australia, Bangladesh, England, South Africa, USA, West Indies); then "the next best-ranked teams" at 30 Jun 2024 not in the top seven: Ireland, New Zealand, Pakistan. Arab News (3 Jul 2024): Pakistan 7th, New Zealand 6th, Ireland 11th at the 30 June cut-off. Remaining eight via regional qualifiers.
- Rankings around the cut-off (Wisden, 15 Jun 2024 table): 9 Bangladesh 226, 10 Afghanistan 220, 11 Ireland 195, 12 Scotland 192 / Zimbabwe 192 (tied), 14 Namibia 189, 15 Netherlands 185. Post-cut-off reporting: "12th-ranked Zimbabwe will miss out due to the USA's surprise progress", so at 30 June Zimbabwe were 12th and Scotland 13th (Scotland had lost to Australia in the interval).
- Rule 2: without Afghanistan in the top seven, a fourth rankings place opens; the next team after Ireland/NZ/Pakistan is Zimbabwe (12th).
- Cascade: Zimbabwe qualified anyway by winning the 2025 Africa Regional Final (beat Namibia by 7 wickets in the final; Namibia also qualified as runner-up). With Zimbabwe taking a direct slot, the Africa slot passes to 3rd-placed Kenya (beat Tanzania by 51 runs in the 3rd-place play-off; standings 1 Zimbabwe, 2 Namibia, 3 Kenya, 4 Tanzania, 5 Uganda, 6 Nigeria, 7 Malawi, 8 Botswana).
- Runner-up choice: Scotland (13th at the cut-off). The ICC's real-world precedent supports a rankings-based replacement: when Bangladesh withdrew in Jan 2026, Scotland were added as "the next highest ranked team in the T20I Rankings". But because Scotland are therefore already in the actual 2026 field, using them as the Afghanistan stand-in only works if your counterfactual field keeps Bangladesh. If your field mirrors the real one (Scotland in for Bangladesh), use Kenya.
- Note: Scotland finished 4th in the 2025 Europe Regional Final (1 Netherlands, 2 Italy, 3 Jersey, 4 Scotland), so an Ireland-based Europe cascade is not in play here.
