# Practitioner cricket simulation engines: commercial managers, browser games, open-source hobby projects, and broadcast forecasting models

Scope note: this covers how *practitioners* (game studios, hobbyists, analytics firms) model ball-by-ball outcomes and player attributes, as a complement to the academic literature. State of play as of October 2026. Throughout, "DEV" marks a confirmed developer statement, "FAN" marks community observation/speculation, and every piece of code carries its licence as reported by the GitHub API on 2026-10-11 (NONE = no licence file = all rights reserved by default, not reusable).

## Key question 1: Cricket Captain / International Cricket Captain (Childish Things, Chris Child) — attributes, ball resolution, rating generation, ageing/youth/form

### Takeaway
Cricket Captain exposes a small, data-derived attribute set (batting, bowling, fielding, aggression, opener type, plus form and per-format ratings) and the developers have confirmed that aggression is now computed directly from real strike rates and that the engine is tuned by "simulating thousands of matches from every situation"; but there is no public developer description of the per-ball resolution formula, and most of what is written about form, regens, ageing and fitness is fan observation.

### Cited Findings
**Origins and design philosophy**
- The ICC engine began as Chris Child's university project; Giant Bomb's history states it was "the final year dissertation project of Chris Child at Birmingham University in 1992" and describes it as a run-scoring engine, with Empire producer Brian Walker quoted: "We wanted a game where the player would feel that his decisions were going to have a strong bearing on the matches" (secondary source, quoting Walker) — [Giant Bomb](https://giantbomb.com/wiki/Games/International_Cricket_Captain); Wikipedia confirms Child "programmed the original game engine as a university project" and that Walker "produced and co-designed the game", with the series inspired by Championship Manager — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Captain)
- ICC III (6 July 2007) introduced "a new algorithmic method of calculating player performances", which Wikipedia attributes to competitive pressure from the Cricket Coach series — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Captain)
- Childish Things has been sole developer since ICC 2009 (after Empire went into administration); publisher KISS Ltd from 2016 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Captain)

**Exposed player attributes (DEV)**
- Cricket Captain 2025's Player Editor "allows for editing player abilities, including batting, bowling, fielding, aggression and opener type" (Steam store page, Childish Things Ltd, released 27 June 2025) — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3757420/)
- CC2025 also states the 20-over engine was updated "to rebalance scoring rates and wicket chances" and that Player Generation was "rebalanced with a focus on 20 Over skill" — [Steam](https://store.steampowered.com/app/3757420/)
- CC2024 (Sureshot, 26 May 2024): "with feedback from last year's game, and simulating thousands of matches from every situation, we have rebalanced scoring rates across the board, with focus on both First Class and limited over scoring rates and AI"; "Improved Player Generation ... rebalanced with focus on player aggression"; database of 7,500+ players with "Real-world player form for domestic and international cricket" and a new in-match "Score Predictor" (score and win predictor) — [Childish Things forum, CC24 features](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2024/82523-cricket-captain-2024-release-date-and-features)
- CC2023 feature list: "Match Engine: new Bazball inspired player aggression and rating system"; aggression is set "from their batting ability and the tactic currently selected, not from the format being played"; "Player Form: includes historical data, opposition and an updated system"; wicketkeeper stats (catches, byes) added — [Nintendo CC2023 listing](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/cricket-captain-2023-switch/)

**How aggression is derived from data (DEV, the most detailed engine disclosure found)**
- Sureshot (Administrator, 12 Jun 2022): "All aggression rates are now determined directly from data using a player's strike rate." First-class bands: >63% very aggressive, <63 aggressive, <56 normal, <50 defensive, <45 very defensive. "For first class cricket this is directly taken from the data." "For OD/T20 players we take a weighted average based on their strike rate in OD and T20 matches", with weightings relative to format averages (T20 average 126%, OD average 83%). "The aggression label is much less fixed than it was in 2021." — [Childish Things forum, aggression thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2022/78888-new-aggression-rating-system-explained)
- Chris Child (posting as "Dr Cricket", 15 Jun 2022) on coaching: "Aggressive and Defensive training mainly effects FC SR, with some effect on OD at lower aggression levels"; "Boundary hitting and OD accumulator mainly effect higher OD SR". Sureshot (21 Jun 2022): FC and limited-overs aggression "are treated separately and can be improved separately." Moving more than one aggression level through training "would be rare, rather than the norm, but possible." — [same thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2022/78888-new-aggression-rating-system-explained)
- Sureshot (22 Jun 2024): aggression labels "are now automatically assigned based on player data compared against global data"; before 2021 "a player's aggression (base SR) was determined by their labelled type"; limited-overs aggression is "an aggregate of both, but weighted towards 20 over" — [CC24 database feedback thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2024/82879-cc24-database-feedback)
- CC2022 feature announcement: aggression reworked for FC and LO so the system "mirrors a player's real strike rate, modelling boundary hitting and accumulation ability"; "batters settle more quickly, making collapses less prevalent"; a separate change "reduces batting collapses in 20 Over games" — [CC2022 features thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2022/78466-cricket-captain-2022-features-and-steam-page)
- CC2021 patch 21.03 note: developer "reduced incoming FC batsman aggression to 2" and "improved FC batting for draw AI" — evidence that incoming-batter aggression is a tunable engine parameter — [CC2021 patch thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2021/77868-cricket-captain-2021-version-21-03-released-steam-and-21-23-mac-ios-and-android-coming-soon)

**Rating generation for real players (DEV fragments + FAN)**
- In the CC24 database thread Sureshot defends ratings by reference to career figures ("His ability is fair against his record"; Pat Brown "Going at 9.5 in his career") and notes a fringe player's "ability can vary significantly between saves" (i.e., some randomisation of ability at save creation). User Lynx54321 (24 Jul 2024) counters that "there should be more recency bias" and ability should reflect "this season not just career records" — [CC24 database feedback](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2024/82879-cc24-database-feedback)
- A fan post in the aggression thread (Saadbukhari87, 16 Jun 2022, FAN) says technique coaching is the route to underlying skill improvement, while other coaching options mainly shift strike rate and scoring preferences against pace/spin and on/off side. The existence of pace-vs-spin and on/off-side scoring preferences is consistent with developer remarks about "scoring area" training but the mechanism is not developer-confirmed — [aggression thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2022/78888-new-aggression-rating-system-explained)

**Ageing, youth generation, form (FAN unless noted)**
- Regens are "new players with new names, not the same players just younger"; players report the best young talent appears "when 2 or 3 ppl retire" and that developers "toned back the quality of regens in a past release" — [Youth players thread, CC2018](https://childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2018/64860-youth-players)
- Community heuristics for spotting regens: second-XI batting average above ~55 or bowling average under ~20; low-average youngsters "can develop in to a future super stars after 5 years or so" — [How to spot promising regens](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2018/63775-how-to-spot-promising-regens)
- Ageing: a player reports "by age 34, players start to regress big time" — [Retirement age thread](https://childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2016/60702-what-s-the-ideal-average-retirement-age-for-your-players)
- Fitness/injury complaints (harrys22, 9 Jul 2024): "injuries are extremely unrealistic", "Fast bowlers shouldn't be able to bowl 40 overs every test and never get hurt", asks for "some sort of measure of player fitness or player mindset" — [CC24 database feedback](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2024/82879-cc24-database-feedback)

### Inferences
- The engine appears to be a parametric model keyed on per-player strike rate (aggression), ability, and format-specific ratings, with pitch/weather and tactic modifiers; the developers tune it by brute-force simulation from many match states rather than by fitting to ball-by-ball data. The 2022–2025 trend is to replace hand-labelled categories (aggression type) with values read directly from real statistics.
- "Ability can vary significantly between saves" implies per-save noise added to borderline players' hidden ability — a cheap way to create replay variety.

### Gaps
- No developer interview or post describes the per-ball probability function (how batting ability, bowling ability, aggression, pitch and match state combine). PlanetCricket threads that might contain fan reverse-engineering returned HTTP 403 and could not be read.
- No source documents the ageing curve, youth-generation algorithm or how real-world "form" data is converted into an in-game modifier.

## Key question 2: Cricket Coach 2013/2014 (Rockingham Software, Oli Norwell)

### Takeaway
Cricket Coach's developers said publicly that the engine was built so results are "not as predictable as just a set of skill numbers" with form, conditions and strategy playing a large role, and CC2014 added "weighting" code so minnow matches simulate realistically; no post-mortem or engine blog was found and the series appears dormant after a never-completed rewrite.

### Cited Findings
- 2005 Cricket Web interview (Norwell and Glenn Stiemens): the match engine was "rewritten from scratch" rather than carried over from Michael Vaughan's Cricket Manager; Norwell: "we made sure the match engine isn't as predictable as just a set of skill numbers. Form, conditions and strategy all play a huge role in the outcome" — [Cricket Web, Sept 2005 interview](https://www.cricketweb.net/?p=2272)
- 2003 interview: stated goal was "the excitement of arcade action and the realism of an accurate simulation"; viewers could watch every ball, highlights, or just statistics — [Cricket Web, Feb 2003 interview](https://www.cricketweb.net/game/cricket-coach/february-03-interview/)
- Cricket Coach 2014 feature list: "new special 'weighting' code in match engine to allow minnows' games to be realistically simulated" and an "Entirely re-written international rankings engine" — [Cricket Web, Cricket Coach 2014](https://www.cricketweb.net/game/cricket-coach-2014)
- Cricket Coach 2009 v3.05 changelog contained the line "Fixed match engine so realistic scores are now achieved"; a bowling-plan editor (per-bowler plans) existed and had save/load fixes in a later release — [PlanetCricket, CC2009 v3.09 thread](https://www.planetcricket.org/forums/threads/cricket-coach-2009-v3-09-new-version-released.60838)
- GM Games profile: Cricket Coach 2014 "is primarily the product of over 14 years of work by Oli Norwell"; Rockingham said the game "is currently being completely rewritten to support a wider range of devices" and a Cricket Coach 3 was planned for Steam Early Access — [GM Games, Oli Norwell](https://gmgames.org/developer/oli-norwell/)
- Wikipedia credits Cricket Coach's competition with pushing Cricket Captain to its 2007 "new algorithmic method" — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Captain)

### Inferences
- Cricket Coach's selling point against Cricket Captain was a deeper tactics layer (bowling plans, per-ball viewing) and explicit form/conditions modifiers; the "weighting code for minnows" suggests the base engine normalised outcomes around a global average and needed a team-strength scaling term to stop weak sides over-performing.

### Gaps
- The PlanetCricket developer interview (thread 14765) and Oli Norwell's posts returned HTTP 403; no engine design blog or post-mortem was found. No source lists Cricket Coach's player attribute names.

## Key question 3: Other management titles — Battrick, From The Pavilion, PlanetCricket hobby managers, Cricket Heroes, Cricket Manager

### Takeaway
Browser managers (Battrick, From The Pavilion) use ~7–12 named skills on word-ladder scales (atrocious → superb) plus form, fatigue/PFL, experience and explicit per-player aggression orders, with pitch types tied to bowler types; newer hobby managers on PlanetCricket favour trait systems and claim data-trained engines, but none publish their resolution logic.

### Cited Findings
**Battrick (Hattrick-style, launched mid-2000s; official rules)**
- Seven main skills: Stamina, Batting, Bowling, Wicket Keeping, Concentration, Consistency, Fielding; plus Aggression, Experience, Leadership, Form (separate batting and bowling form), and Personal Fitness Level (PFL). Top level label is "superb" — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=0)
- Per-player orders: attacking/normal/defensive for batters and bowlers. "The more aggressive a player, the more likely they are to score runs quickly as a batter, but they will be more likely to get out"; aggressive bowlers take more wickets but are more expensive. Players may deviate from orders, more likely with high Experience — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=0)
- First-class bowling: per bowler you set priority, max/min overs per spell, and max run-rate per spell; "Your captain may override these based on performance" — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=0)
- Seven pitch types with favoured bowler type: Uneven/Cracked/Green → seam; Hard and Fast/Flat → batters; Dusty → spin; Slow → all bowlers. Weather is regional, never rains, and "certain types of player will flourish in certain weather conditions"; "A player's form has a significant impact on their performance"; stamina reduces tiring and speeds PFL recovery; morale affects performance — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=0)
- Training is capped at ten "nets" per week; a reviewer says the player base is "steadily shrinking ... as a result of the lack of fresh development" — [PlanetCricket post](https://www.planetcricket.org/forums/posts/2917950/)

**From The Pavilion (free browser manager)**
- Player profile attributes visible in forum discussion: Batting, Bowling, Keeping, Fielding, plus sub-attributes Endurance, Technique, Power; status fields Form, Fatigue, Experience, Captaincy; talents such as Swing, Accumulator, Sturdy, Gifted; label ladder atrocious, dreadful, poor, ordinary, average, reasonable, capable, reliable, accomplished, expert, outstanding; an overall numeric "rating" (e.g., 38,415) and wage — [FTP forum, How to Train?](https://www.fromthepavilion.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=1391358)
- A player observed that a "skill summary" rating can exceed the underlying batting skill, with others attributing the gap to experience and technique (FAN) — [FTP forum](https://www.fromthepavilion.org/forums/viewtopic.php?p=1391358)
- Comparison by a user: FTP's bowling orders are "a lot better than Battrick, with way more control" though "very simple" — [PlanetCricket post](https://www.planetcricket.org/forums/posts/2917950/)

**PlanetCricket hobby managers (self-reported by developers)**
- Cricket Manager 24 sim: "24 different player roles", "ball-by-ball simulation in which each ball's outcome is influenced by the skills of the players involved", and "skill normalization after each season to maintain realistic distribution" — [PlanetCricket, Cricket Manager 24](https://www.planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=3644116)
- Pro Cricket Manager 2025 3D (same developer): trait system (e.g., "Finisher" for death overs, "Golden Arm" for wicket-taking); claims the engine is "trained on over 100k international matches data from cricinfo" in one post and "every single T20 match played between top 12 international teams" in another (inconsistent, unverified); "Pitch impacts significantly impact match outcome"; extreme pitch ratings make an "unplayable pitch", recommended range 4–7 — [PlanetCricket, PCM 2025 alpha](https://www.planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=3663102); [PlanetCricket, PCM 2025 alpha releasing](https://planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=3670139)
- SimCricketX (Manish, Python web app, T20, rain interruptions): repo at github.com/Manishrdy/SimCricketX per the forum; no licence stated in forum or search results — [PlanetCricket, SimCricketX release](https://planetcricket.org/forums/threads/released-simcricketx-v1-0-complete-t20-experience-with-realistic-match-engine-free-to-play.122749)
- Cricket Heroes (Kurt Kruger): "every aspect of the game is modeled to be procedural - nothing is scripted"; "ball deformation and discoloration directly affects the ball physics"; venue weather profiles affect ball physics and player models; developer: ball condition "will deteriorate (faster in ODIs than tests). This has a minor bearing on things like swing and seam" — [ModDB, Cricket Heroes](https://www.moddb.com/games/cricket-heroes); [PlanetCricket, Cricket Heroes testing](https://planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=2317252)
- "Cricket Manager" (CricketManager org, MIT-licensed public info repo, last push Oct 2024) publishes only a roadmap, no engine code — [GitHub CricketManager/CricketManager](https://github.com/CricketManager/CricketManager)

### Inferences
- The browser-manager consensus attribute model is: 4 core skills (bat/bowl/keep/field) + 2–3 physical/mental modifiers (stamina/endurance, concentration/technique, power) + dynamic state (form, fatigue, morale, experience) + a per-player aggression dial. Pitch type is handled as a categorical that favours a bowler class rather than as continuous parameters.
- Hobby developers increasingly advertise "trained on real data" engines, but none of the PlanetCricket projects publish validation.

### Gaps
- Could not locate official From The Pavilion rules/manual (search returned only forum threads). Cricket Architect (Cricket Web browser manager) thread was HTTP 403. No independent review of "Cricket Manager Pro 2023/2024" or "Cricket Club Manager" was found; the former name may not correspond to a shipped product.

### Inferences
- For a free non-commercial project, the safest code to study/reuse is MIT (CricVerse, cricfuzz, TestMatch, CricketGameEngine, yorkpy) or AGPL (the-archer, only if the project can accept copyleft). ## Key question 5: Practitioner blogs / notebooks building T20-ODI simulators from Cricsheet (Monte Carlo over per-player frequencies, logistic/multinomial, GBM) and reported realism checks

### Takeaway
The best-documented practitioner engine (Andrew Kuo, 2021) feeds batter and bowler historical outcome distributions plus match state into a small neural net that outputs an 8-class per-ball distribution, then Monte-Carlos whole matches; it matched real distributions of totals/wickets and edged bookmaker log-loss (0.687 vs 0.695) but with only 55.6% winner accuracy — the recurring lesson is that per-ball models recover realism but T20 winner prediction stays near coin-flip.

### Cited Findings
- Andrew Kuo, "Predicting T20 Cricket Matches With a Ball Simulation Model" (TDS, 18 Jan 2021): Keras feed-forward net, two dense layers of 50 (ReLU, batchnorm, dropout), 8 outputs (0,1,2,3,4,6,W,Wide), ~4,508 parameters. Inputs: innings, over, runs, wickets, first-innings score, plus each bowler's and batter's historical ball-outcome distribution (no explicit rating/Elo). Simulation: coin toss, then sample each ball from the predicted distribution and update state; ~10k simulations/hour; 500 sims per match in evaluation. Data: 677,291 balls, 3,651 T20 matches, 2,255 players, seven leagues 2003–2020 (private, not shared). Realism: simulated first-innings totals, match wickets and highest scores compared visually/statistically with real matches "and the distributions were similar". Results over 1,126 matches: 55.6% accuracy, log loss 0.687 vs Bet365 54.2% / 0.695 (a constant 0.5 gives 0.693); first-innings score correlation 0.305, mean simulated SD 28.6; top run-scorer correct 25% (in top 4 ~80%); top wicket-taker 35% (top 3 ~75%). No Brier/calibration; code not published, no licence — [Towards Data Science](https://towardsdatascience.com/predicting-t20-cricket-matches-with-a-ball-simulation-model-1e9cae5dea22)
- Tejal Narkar, "Cricket simulation engine using machine learning" (Medium): Cricsheet ODI JSON flattened to one row per ball; features include batter/bowler aggregates (balls faced, runs given, wickets), required run rate, last-30-ball scoring; random-forest classifier over dot/1/2/3/4/6 and six dismissal types; author notes simpler approaches "do not take aspects like pitch, match context (innings, required run rate, match state etc) into account". Code: the-archer/cricket-simulator, AGPL-3.0 — [Medium](https://medium.com/@tejalnarkar/cricket-simulation-engine-using-machine-learning-a2758933b0a7); [GitHub](https://github.com/the-archer/cricket-simulator)
- Part Time Analyst, "Twenty20 Win Probability Added" (R-bloggers, 20 Jun 2020): Cricsheet IPL/international/BBL/Blast/PSL, 600k+ balls; tidymodels logistic regression, separate models per innings; features balls faced, runs, wickets, plus runs required in the second innings; ROC evaluation; second-innings model discriminates better; no code repo/licence — [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2020/06/twenty20-win-probability-added/)
- Tinniam V Ganesh, "Computing Win-Probability of T20 matches" (R-bloggers, 24 Dec 2022): nine Cricsheet T20 leagues, ~1.2M rows; features ballNum, ballsRemaining, runs, runRate, numWickets, runsMomentum = (11 − wickets)/balls remaining, perfIndex = run rate × wickets in hand; logistic ~0.64, linear SVC 0.52, Keras DL ~0.73 after feature engineering, ranger random forest 0.739 accuracy / AUC 0.837; "Performance Index had the highest importance, while wickets were less important than he expected"; no licence stated — [R-bloggers](https://www.r-bloggers.com/2022/12/computing-win-probability-of-t20-matches/)
- A Cricketing View, "Runs Above Average Replacement (RAAR) – In T20" (Substack, 29 Apr 2025): expected runs per delivery modelled as a function of (balls faced by batter, balls remaining, wickets in hand, scoring rate at start of delivery) via DNN regression; RAAR = actual − xR; extras other than byes/leg-byes count against bowlers; e.g., Iyer 82(36) = +23.9 — [Cricketing View](https://cricketingview.substack.com/p/runs-above-average-replacement-raar)
- MrAnayDongre's repo implements the Swartz-style discrete generator conditional on batter, bowler, wickets lost, balls bowled and innings — [GitHub](https://github.com/MrAnayDongre/Modelling-and-simulation-for-one-day-cricket)
- The kanchanpotdar notebook argues "Standard statistical summaries (average score, win rate) fail to capture the sequential dependency between consecutive deliveries" and uses Dirichlet-perturbed transition matrices for match-to-match variance — [GitHub](https://github.com/kanchanpotdar/Monte-Carlo-Simulation-of-Cricket-Match-Outcomes)

### Inferences
- Practitioner engines converge on one architecture: per-ball multinomial over {0,1,2,3,4,6,W,extras} conditioned on (batter profile, bowler profile, phase/over, wickets, innings/target), sampled forward. Encoding players as their *empirical outcome distribution* (Kuo) is a cheap, interpretable alternative to learned embeddings and maps naturally onto card attributes.
- Realism checks that practitioners actually run: distribution of first-innings totals, wickets per match, highest individual score, first-innings SD (~28–30 in T20), top-scorer/top-wicket-taker hit rates. None report calibration curves.

### Gaps
- No practitioner post was found that models outcomes with gradient-boosted trees at the ball level; GBM use is confined to match-winner prediction. No Kaggle notebook combining Cricsheet, per-player ball probabilities and full-match simulation was found.

## Key question 6: CricViz, Hawk-Eye and ESPNcricinfo forecasting — public knowledge of win-probability / expected-runs models and features

### Takeaway
CricViz has said WinViz runs simulations driven primarily by player career records plus venue history and (since 2019) ball-tracking-derived pitch descriptors, and since June 2023 is "powered by AI and machine learning" with conditional what-if queries; ESPNcricinfo's Forecaster (with IIT Madras) uses run rate, overs/wickets left and player quality/form; Hawk-Eye publishes nothing about a forecasting model.

### Cited Findings
**CricViz**
- WinViz "takes account of the players, the venue and the state of the match to run a series of simulations" and ascribes each team a win percentage; database layers go from scorecards to a ball-by-ball digital archive — [Swarajya interview, 31 Jul 2019](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/sports%2Fswarajya-speaks-to-cricviz-the-team-behind-the-data-intelligence-models-for-cricket-world-cup-2019)
- "The player's record is the more important variable when generating the predictor models"; players simulated "based on their career numbers"; "A team's previous record against a particular opposition is something that isn't considered"; historical venue data gives a par score per ground; models are adjusted for trends such as bat-first advantage; in Tests the "resources in hand" idea is less volatile than in limited overs — [Swarajya, 2019](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/sports%2Fswarajya-speaks-to-cricviz-the-team-behind-the-data-intelligence-models-for-cricket-world-cup-2019)
- PitchViz: "We are able to calculate a number using ball tracking data that includes the amount of seam, swing, spin, bounce", used to find past pitches with similar behaviour and compare player performance on them — [Swarajya, 2019](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/sports%2Fswarajya-speaks-to-cricviz-the-team-behind-the-data-intelligence-models-for-cricket-world-cup-2019)
- 15 Jun 2023 press release (Ellipse Data): WinViz "Now powered by AI and machine learning", updated because of faster Test scoring; new "conditional probability element" answering e.g. how WinViz changes if two wickets fall or a side declares; used by ICC, Sky, Fox, CWI, Wisden; James Norton-Brown is Head of Data Science — [Ellipse Data](https://ellipsedata.com/?p=39772)
- Match Impact measures a player's contribution to win probability; example: at 70/3 India's top three are their "most valuable resources" — [Swarajya, 2019](https://swarajyamag.com/amp/story/sports%2Fswarajya-speaks-to-cricviz-the-team-behind-the-data-intelligence-models-for-cricket-world-cup-2019)

**ESPNcricinfo**
- Superstats (2019, with IIT Madras and Gyan Data) includes a Forecaster predicting final innings score and win probability "using statistical and machine learning models"; inputs "the current run rate, number of overs and wickets left, quality and form of the players" — [Punekar News](https://www.punekarnews.in/espncricinfo-launches-superstats-new-metrics-that-uses-data-science-to-analyze-the-game-of-cricket/)
- Smart Stats contextualise performance by "pitch conditions, quality of opposition, and match situation - in terms of the pressure on the player"; Smart Strike Rate compares a batter's rate with the match-wide and other-end scoring rates — [ESPN, Smart Stats explainer](https://www.espn.co.uk/cricket/story/_/id/26312448/hd-version-conventional-cricket-stats)

**WASP (precedent for expected-runs)**
- WASP asks "what would happen if the average team batting against the average bowling team on this pitch was to do its average performance from this point on", via a recursive formula over balls bowled and wickets lost, fitted to ODIs from 2006 — [Otago Daily Times](https://www.odt.co.nz/node/330932)

**Hawk-Eye**
- Only ball-tracking is described publicly (multi-camera triangulation, 3D trajectory reconstruction); no win-predictor methodology found — [It's Only Cricket](https://www.itsonlycricket.com/cricket-ball-tracking)

### Inferences
- The broadcast models are simulation-based like the hobby engines, but with (a) player strength from career records shrunk toward averages, (b) venue par scores, and (c) a continuous pitch descriptor from tracking data. A card game cannot reproduce (c) but can mimic it with a per-match pitch vector (seam, swing, spin, bounce) that scales bowler-type effectiveness.

### Gaps
- No public document gives WinViz's simulation algorithm, feature weights, calibration, or the post-2023 ML architecture. Forecaster's algorithm, training window and accuracy are unpublished. Hawk-Eye's forecasting (if any) is undocumented.

## Key question 7: Design lessons — fun and fairness, randomness, making aggression and bowling changes matter, declaration AI, the "coin flip" criticism

### Takeaway
Community and critic feedback on Cricket Captain says the engine is "a giant spreadsheet steeped in RNG" where randomness is both the fun and the frustration; players want aggression to have legible, reliable effects, field/bowling changes to visibly matter, a distinct limited-overs engine with strike rotation, and AI that declares and chases sensibly — and developers have responded mainly by tuning collapses, aggression derivation and scoring rates rather than exposing more levers.

### Cited Findings
**The RNG / coin-flip critique**
- Kotaku Australia: "At its heart, Cricket Captain is a giant spreadsheet steeped in RNG"; if the game decides Pakistan's openers will "tonk you for a double century ... tonk away they will" and "that's part of the fun"; but the player can still "cock-up the match itself with bad field placings, have crappy part-timers bowl too many overs, or just fail to respond to how the opposition are playing"; negative Steam reviewers with hundreds of hours "have a point" — [Kotaku Australia](https://www.kotaku.com.au/?p=908039)
- Cricket Web's ICC 2014 review cites "very strange and often unrealistic examples of cricket" — six wickets in six balls in a county game, 7/18 in four overs — while crediting engine reworkings for refreshing the game — [Cricket Web, ICC 2014 review](https://www.cricketweb.net/?p=11862)
- The Cricketer's review of a recent edition: "seemingly fool-proof tactics fail spectacularly", too much or too little aggression leads to collapses or dry spells, and "there are too few options to play with to change the course of a match" — [The Cricketer](https://www.thecricketer.com/topics/features/cricket_captain's_latest_iteration_remains_an_enjoyable_way_to_lose_track_of_time.html)
- A PlanetCricket poster called a T20 engine result "absolutely batshit" after a last-wicket stand nearly tripled the score — [PlanetCricket](https://planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=3407283)
- Counterpoint: a CC2021 review says "the match engine continues to improve and it's a seriously good representation of cricket now" — [Childish Things forum, CC21 review](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2021/76563-cc21-review)
- CricVerse's README states the design response explicitly: "Calibrated, not random: outcome weights are Monte-Carlo tuned so skill decides matches - no freak 250s or 30-all-out collapses between equal sides" — [GitHub CricVerse](https://github.com/TheJaiv/CricVerse)

**Making aggression matter (and be legible)**
- Battrick's rule text gives the canonical trade-off players expect: more aggressive batting = faster runs but more likely out; aggressive bowling = more wickets but more expensive — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=0)
- Cricket Captain moved from label-based aggression to strike-rate-derived aggression (2022), made aggression respond to the current batting tactic rather than format (2023), and tuned "batters settle more quickly, making collapses less prevalent" — DEV, see Q1 sources — [aggression thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2022/78888-new-aggression-rating-system-explained); [CC2022 features](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2022/78466-cricket-captain-2022-features-and-steam-page)
- After CC2024's rebalance, a user (muzzy) preferred the 2023 engine "because batters can keep attacking after losing a wicket" and found big scores harder — evidence that players notice and dislike when aggression is throttled by wickets — [CC24 features thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2024/82523-cricket-captain-2024-release-date-and-features)
- A Cricket Coach developer stated the goal that results not be "as predictable as just a set of skill numbers" with form, conditions and strategy playing "a huge role" — [Cricket Web 2005](https://www.cricketweb.net/?p=2272)

**Bowling changes, fields, limited-overs engine, AI chases/declarations (FAN)**
- CC2020 request thread: run chases "end too early, often between 30 and 40 overs"; AI loses 3–4 wickets in the first 10 of a 300+ chase and keeps attacking at 20/4; "too few singles, too many dot balls, too many boundaries in the middle overs"; "I feel the match engine used in limited overs is the extension of test matches"; another: "It's impossible to keep the scoreboard 'ticking' by rotating the strike"; ODI fields "have not changed since ICC 2000"; coaching, ranking, field settings and match conditions called "the most stale and outdated components"; D/L and improved weather in CC20 praised as adding realism; no developer replies on the page — [CC2020 fans thread](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/cricket-captain-2020/69667-things-that-fans-want-to-see-in-cc2020)
- Field settings: default fields are linked to the bowler's aggression; even at aggression zero "there are still 6 fielders in the ring" — [PlanetCricket, CC2012 help](https://planetcricket.org/forums/threads/help-with-cricket-captain-2012-how-do-i-play-better.82718); custom field layouts stopped working as well after AI tweaks "counter-acting an off-side field" — [Childish Things, field placings](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2018/64516-field-placings)
- Declarations: the AI "usually bats to a score of 600 before declaring" (CC2012); suggestion that declaration AI weigh series situation (a side 1-0 down should set sporting declarations) — [PlanetCricket, CC2012 help](https://planetcricket.org/forums/threads/help-with-cricket-captain-2012-how-do-i-play-better.82718); [PlanetCricket, Things the AI should do](https://planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=775785)
- Bowling-change AI wish list: choose by pitch type, stamina, overs left and current form; take off a spinner being hit; avoid part-timers at first change; give death overs to the best bowlers — [PlanetCricket, Things the AI should do](https://planetcricket.org/forums/goto/post?id=775785)
- Fitness/injury realism and recency bias in ratings are recurring 2024 requests — [CC24 database feedback](https://www.childishthings.com/forum/forum/current-and-future-games/cricket-captain-2024/82879-cc24-database-feedback)
- Battrick lets the AI captain "override" user bowling plans based on performance, and makes order-deviation a function of Experience — a built-in explanation layer for when plans are not followed — [Battrick rules](https://www.battrick.org/nl/rules.asp?item=0)

**Football Manager parallels cited by cricket developers**
- Wikipedia notes ICC was inspired by Championship Manager and shared its producer (Brian Walker) — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Captain). No cricket developer was found citing FM's engine design specifically.

### Inferences
- Lessons for a card/sim hybrid: (1) bound the tails — Monte-Carlo-tune so equal sides rarely produce freak totals (CricVerse) and dampen early-innings collapse hazard (CC2022); (2) make aggression a visible dial with a monotone, explainable run-rate/dismissal trade-off (Battrick rule text) that is driven by the chosen tactic rather than format (CC2023); (3) give limited-overs its own strike-rotation/singles model, since "extension of Test engine" was the top CC complaint; (4) tie default fields and bowling-change AI to pitch type, stamina and form so player decisions have a readable counterfactual; (5) show a score/win predictor in-match (CC2024) so the player can see decisions move probabilities — the broadcast WinViz pattern applied to game feedback.

### Gaps
- No developer (Childish Things, Rockingham, or hobby) has published a reflective post-mortem on randomness vs. agency; everything on this question is critic/fan commentary plus terse patch notes. PlanetCricket threads were largely HTTP 403, so some fan reverse-engineering may exist that this note could not read.
